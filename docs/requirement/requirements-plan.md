# AMX Requirements Plan

**Project:** AMX — Arba Minch Experiences
**Phase:** Phase 2 — Requirements Engineering
**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Draft

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how AMX requirements will be identified, collected, documented, validated, approved, prioritized, traced, and changed.

The objective is to ensure that the AMX system solves real business and user problems before implementation begins.

## 2. Requirements Objectives

The requirements process will:

* Understand the real tourism coordination workflow.
* Identify stakeholder needs and problems.
* Define clear business and system requirements.
* Separate MVP requirements from future features.
* Ensure requirements are feasible and testable.
* Provide traceability from business needs to testing.
* Control changes during development.

## 3. Requirements Sources

Requirements will be collected from:

* AMX business goals and Project Charter.
* Potential visitors and customers.
* Tour guides.
* Drivers.
* Hotels and lodges.
* Boat-service providers.
* Tour operators.
* AMX operational processes.
* Legal and regulatory considerations.
* Real-world tourism coordination practices.

## 4. Requirements Elicitation Methods

| Method             | Purpose                                        |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Interviews         | Understand stakeholder needs and problems      |
| Observation        | Understand current coordination processes      |
| Questionnaires     | Collect visitor preferences                    |
| Process analysis   | Identify workflow and operational requirements |
| Document review    | Identify business and legal constraints        |
| Prototype feedback | Validate proposed system behavior              |

* Booking confirmation.

## 7. Requirement Identification

Requirements will use unique identifiers:

* BR — Business Requirement
* UR — User Requirement
* FR — Functional Requirement
* NFR — Non-Functional Requirement
* BRL — Business Rule
* UC — Use Case
* US — User Story
* TC — Test Case

Example:

`BR-01`, `FR-01`, `NFR-01`, `UC-01`

## 8. Requirement Quality Criteria

Every approved requirement should be:

* Clear
* Necessary
* Feasible
* Consistent
* Testable
* Traceable
* Within the approved scope

## 9. Requirement Prioritization

Requirements will use the MoSCoW method:

* **Must Have** — required for MVP.
* **Should Have** — important but not essential.
* **Could Have** — useful if resources allow.
* **Won't Have** — intentionally excluded from MVP.

## 10. Validation and Approval

Requirements will be reviewed against:

* Business objectives.
* Stakeholder needs.
* MVP scope.
* Technical feasibility.
* Operational feasibility.
* Legal considerations.
* Testability.

A requirement becomes approved only after validation by the AMX project owner.

## 11. Change Management

New requirements after approval must be evaluated for:

* Business value.
* MVP necessity.
* Development effort.
* Cost.
* Schedule impact.
* Security impact.
* Operational impact.

Approved changes must update all affected requirements, design, implementation, and testing documents.

## 12. Requirements Traceability

Important requirements will be traced through:

**Business Requirement → User Requirement → Functional Requirement → Use Case → Design/API/Database → Implementation → Test Case**

## 13. Deliverables

The Requirements Engineering phase will produce:

* Business requirements

* Functional requirements

## 14. Phase Completion Criteria

Requirements Planning is complete when:

* Requirements sources are identified.
* Stakeholders are identified.
* Elicitation methods are defined.
* Requirement standards are defined.
* Prioritization method is defined.
* Validation and approval process is defined.
* Change-control process is defined.
